Which part of horticulture studies fruits?

Explanation: Pomology is the specific branch of horticulture focused on fruit cultivation.

SECTION B (70 MARKS)
QUESTION 3 (10 Marks)
Agriculture is a cornerstone of Tanzania's economy. Justify this statement by explaining five points.
Answer:
Food Security: Agriculture provides staple food for the majority of the population, ensuring national health and reducing
reliance on food imports.
Employment Creation: It provides direct and indirect employment to a major portion of Tanzania's labor force in farming,
processing, and trade.
Source of Raw Materials: Supplies key raw materials to agro-based industries (e.g., textiles, edible oils, grain milling, tea/
coffee processing).
Foreign Exchange Earnings: Export of cash crops like cashew nuts, coffee, tea, cotton, and tobacco brings vital foreign
revenue.
Contribution to GDP: Accounts for a substantial percentage of Tanzania's Gross Domestic Product and drives rural
development.

Agriculture subject teaches about crop and animal production or value chains for better returns. Learning proper practices in
these chains requires knowledge and skills from agriculture and other subjects. Explain how agriculture is closely related with
five other subjects in various ways or fields.
Answer:
Biology: Helps in understanding plant/animal structures, growth processes, genetics, crop pests, diseases, and
photosynthesis.
Chemistry: Essential for understanding soil pH, plant nutrients, fertilizer application, and chemical actions of pesticides and
herbicides.
Physics: Applied in farm power, machinery operation, soil water dynamics, irrigation system designs, and weather
observation.
Mathematics / Bookkeeping: Used for calculating seed rates, plant populations, spacing, germination percentages, farm
budgeting, and profit/loss analysis.
Geography: Provides insights into weather/climate, soil patterns, topography, erosion control, and environmental
management.

QUESTION 5 (10 Marks)
(a) Why are horticultural crops considered essential for economic development and nutritional security? Give five points. (5

(b) Farmers at Zayomoyoni village were advised by an agricultural extension officer to start horticultural production in their

QUESTION 7 (10 Marks)
When you dig soil vertically, you can see different layers. This vertical section is called a soil profile.
(a) Draw a well-labeled diagram of a soil profile and give one characteristic of each profile horizon. (8 Marks)
(b) Why is Horizon B considered to be less fertile than Horizon A? (2 Marks)
Answer:
(a) Soil Profile Horizons & Characteristics:
+-------------------------------------------------+
| O HORIZON | Organic Layer (Decomposed residue)|
+-------------------------------------------------+
| A HORIZON | Topsoil (Rich in humus & life) |
+-------------------------------------------------+
| B HORIZON | Subsoil (Mineral accumulation) |
+-------------------------------------------------+
| C HORIZON | Parent Material (Weathered rock) |
+-------------------------------------------------+
| R HORIZON | Bedrock (Unweathered solid rock) |
+-------------------------------------------------+
O Horizon: Surface layer containing fresh and partially decomposed organic plant/animal material.
A Horizon (Topsoil): Dark layer rich in organic humus; biologically active and highly fertile.
B Horizon (Subsoil): Accumulates leached minerals (iron, clay); lighter color with low organic content.
C Horizon (Parent Material): Contains weathered rock fragments with minimal organic/biological activity.
R Horizon (Bedrock): Solid unweathered underlying rock.
(b) Why Horizon B is Less Fertile than Horizon A:
Horizon B contains far less organic matter/humus and reduced biological activity (earthworms, micro-organisms). It also
accumulates heavy clay which can restrict root aeration and growth.
QUESTION 8 (10 Marks)
(a) Soil is used for growing crops; soil is like a plant's home as it offers good conditions for growth. But not all soil is suitable
for growing crops. Give four reasons for this statement. (4 Marks)
(b) How can farmers keep their soil healthy? Give six points. (6 Marks)
Answer:
(a) Reasons Why Not All Soils Are Suitable for Crops:
Extreme Soil pH: Excessive acidity or alkalinity locks up essential nutrients from plant uptake.
Poor Drainage / Waterlogging: Dense clay or compacted soils trap excess water, causing root suffocation.
Nutrient Deficiencies: Lack of key primary, secondary, or micronutrients needed for crop development.
Unfavorable Texture / Shallow Depth: Pure sand loses water too fast, while shallow bedrock limits root growth.
(b) Practices to Keep Soil Healthy:
Applying organic manure (compost, green manure, or farmyard manure).
Practicing crop rotation including leguminous crops to fix nitrogen.
Mulching the soil surface to retain moisture and suppress weeds.
Controlling soil erosion through terracing, contour plowing, and cover crops.
Avoiding over-use of harsh synthetic fertilizers and toxic agrochemicals.
Practicing minimal or zero tillage to protect soil aggregates and earthworms.

QUESTION 10 (15 Marks)
Soil is full of both macro-organisms and micro-organisms. These organisms play various roles in improving soil. Explain four
benefits and three harmful effects of soil organisms.
Answer:
(a) Four Benefits of Soil Organisms (8 Marks):
Decomposition of Organic Matter: Microorganisms (bacteria and fungi) break down dead plants and animals into humus,
releasing plant-available nutrients.
Biological Nitrogen Fixation: Symbiotic bacteria (e.g., Rhizobium in legume root nodules) convert atmospheric nitrogen
into usable nitrates.
Improvement of Soil Structure & Aeration: Macro-organisms like earthworms create tunnels that improve soil drainage,
aeration, and root expansion.
Biological Pest Control: Beneficial soil organisms prey upon or outcompete disease pathogens and harmful pests in the root
zone.
(b) Three Harmful Effects of Soil Organisms (7 Marks):
Causing Crop Diseases: Pathogenic soil microbes (fungi, bacteria) cause serious diseases such as damping-off, root rot, and
bacterial wilt.
Attacking Roots & Seeds: Parasitic soil pests like root-knot nematodes, cutworms, and mole crickets feed on plant roots and
seeds, destroying crops.
Denitrification: Anaerobic soil bacteria convert useful soil nitrates into nitrogen gas in waterlogged soil, reducing soil
fertility.
